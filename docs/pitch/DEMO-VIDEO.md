# Demo video — 3 minutes

About 430 words, roughly 3 minutes at an easy, conversational pace. Each block
is one shot: **what is on screen**, then **what is said**. Demo family phone:
**9811044321** (the Shuklas: Dadi, Prashant, Priya).

Read it like you're telling a friend about your grandmother, not presenting.
Short pauses between blocks so the screen can catch up.

---

### 0:00–0:30 · The problem
**Screen:** Splash screen, then *Hello, family*.

> This is Dadi. She's seventy-one, and she takes three medicines every day.
>
> Her doctor writes them on a slip. One-zero-one. BD. After food. Five days.
> Honestly, most of us can't make sense of that either. Which one is the
> morning tablet? Is it one, or half? Before food, or after?
>
> And then there's the part that worries us most. We're at work. We're in a
> meeting. There's no way to know if Dadi took her eight o'clock tablet. We call,
> she says "haan, le li", and we just hope she did.
>
> So we built Parcha.

### 0:30–1:00 · Adding the prescription
**Screen:** Sign in with 9811044321 → New Parcha → *Printed?* → photo of the slip → confirm each medicine → strip front and back → schedule locked.

> When Dadi gets back from the doctor, you take a photo of her prescription.
> Parcha picks out each medicine and when to take it.
>
> You check each one yourself. If the time isn't written anywhere, Parcha leaves
> it blank and asks you. It never guesses with medicine.
>
> Then you take a photo of each strip. Dadi doesn't remember medicine names, but
> she knows what her tablets look like. That's what she'll see.

### 1:00–1:35 · Dadi's reminder
**Screen:** Dose card with the strip photo → Taken / Not taken → Dadi's simple screen (Hindi voice plays) → record a voice message.

> At eight o'clock, this pops up for Dadi, with a photo of the exact strip she
> needs. She taps Taken. If she taps Not taken, it reminds her again in half an
> hour.
>
> This is Dadi's own screen. Big buttons, and it talks to her in Hindi.
>
> And if something's wrong, she doesn't need to type. She just holds the button
> and says it. "The white tablets are finished."

### 1:35–2:00 · You can see it, from anywhere
**Screen:** Overview feed → Dadi's doses, read-only → history grid.

> And this is what you see, even in the middle of a meeting.
> "Dadi took her BP tablet at 8:33." "Dadi sent you a message."
>
> You can see the whole week at a glance. Green means taken, red means missed.
>
> But only Dadi can mark her own medicine. Nobody can tick it for her, so what
> you see is what really happened.

### 2:00–2:40 · Ask
**Screen:** Ask → "What is due today?" → photo of a strip → answer in Hindi with Listen → blurry photo refused.

> Sometimes there's a new strip in the house and nobody's sure about it. So Dadi,
> or you, can just ask.
>
> Take a photo of the strip. Parcha checks it against Dadi's own prescription,
> and checks the expiry date. Then it answers in Hindi or English, and reads it
> out loud.
>
> If that tablet isn't on her prescription, it tells her not to take it until
> the doctor says so. If it's expired, it says that too.
>
> If the photo is blurry, it asks for a clearer one. It doesn't guess.
>
> And it never acts like a doctor. It won't tell her to start, stop or change a
> medicine.

### 2:40–3:00 · Close
**Screen:** Dadi's screen, then the Parcha logo.

> The doctor's job ends when the prescription is written. That's where Parcha
> begins.
>
> Dadi takes the right tablet, at the right time. And you finally know she did.
>
> Parcha.
